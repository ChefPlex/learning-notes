# Baseline Before You Promise

A TPM lesson from a program I ran early in my consulting years: baseline before you promise.

In 2011 I walked into a gaming company in South San Francisco as project manager and security lead, and it was a lot of stuff going on. They had several games live at once, and program management inside technical operations was out of control. They also needed a full security review.

They wanted the security work started and targets committed. We did operations first. We got them under control and baselined everything: what was running, what state it was in, how we would know when it broke, and what the process was to fix it. Then we agreed SLAs with the business. Before the baseline, nobody knew what was achievable. The security review came second, and I ran it as a program, from corporate networks through to user accounts and privileges in the games.

Three things I learned:

1. **Baseline before you promise.** Once we had a baseline, we knew what we could actually achieve, and we could improve from there.

2. **Order matters.** You cannot secure what you cannot see. Getting operations visible first is what made the security work plannable.

3. **Standardized is often the right place to stop.** We took the function from ad hoc to standardized on a maturity model and stopped there on purpose. Optimizing a process assumes it will last long enough to pay back, and in a business moving that fast, many processes do not. It worked okay, and okay was the right target.

I still work this way. When I set up AI agents for my own work, one of the first permanent rules was measure before you build.

If your team is about to commit to an AI accuracy or automation target, ask to see the baseline first.
