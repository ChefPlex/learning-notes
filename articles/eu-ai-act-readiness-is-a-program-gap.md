# EU AI Act Readiness Is a Program-Management Gap

As of August 2, 2026, the European AI Office can open investigations into general-purpose AI providers, demand technical documentation, order risk mitigations, and levy fines up to the greater of 15 million euros or 3% of worldwide annual turnover. The governance and transparency obligations are no longer "coming." They are in effect.

Here is the pattern I keep seeing: a company has a lawyer reading the statute and no one owning the program. Those are two different jobs. The statute tells you what good looks like. It does not stand up the controls, wire in the evidence, or decide who signs off when a model ships. That gap is a program-management gap, and most orgs do not have an owner for it yet.

If I were standing up AI Act readiness Monday morning, the first five things I would put an owner on:

1. **Risk register for every AI system in scope, mapped to its specific obligations.** Not a spreadsheet that dies in a quarter.
2. **Human-oversight control points.** Where a person can actually intervene, with both the authority and the interface to do it.
3. **Model and data documentation trail.** Provenance, evals, and versioning you can hand a regulator without a fire drill.
4. **Transparency in the product itself.** Users know when they are dealing with a model. That is a design decision, not a footnote.
5. **A clear RACI for sign-off.** Who is accountable when something ships, and who has the authority to stop it.

Four of these five are ownership, evidence, and control points that have to hold under a deadline. Only one is a legal question.

The companies treating this as a program problem now will move faster than the ones treating it as a legal problem in Q4. Curious where this is landing for others: security, legal, product, or a dedicated program owner?
