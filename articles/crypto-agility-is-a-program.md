# Crypto Agility Is a Program, Not an Algorithm

If you own any part of a post-quantum plan, NIST's white paper on crypto agility is worth your time, and most of it is not about cryptography: [Considerations for Achieving Crypto Agility: Strategies and Practices](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/cswp/39/upd1/considerations-for-achieving-crypto-agility/final)

What stuck with me:

These transitions take decades, not quarters. NIST notes that "the complete transition from Triple DES to AES took 23 years." SHA-1 was found weak in 2005, and NIST's date for finishing the move away from it is the end of 2030. This time the change is "much larger in scale," because "all public-key algorithms will need to be replaced rather than just a single algorithm."

It is not only an engineering problem. Crypto agility is "not only a task for product designers, implementors, and operators." The paper names architects, developers, hardware designers and executives, and its plan starts with governance and "a responsible official to lead the effort."

And measure the right thing. NIST's suggested indicators include "the time, cost, and ease to migrate." That is how fast you can change, not how much you have changed. A percent-migrated number mostly rewards doing the easy systems first.

The algorithms are the settled part. Ownership, and how fast you can make the next change, are the program.

If you had to replace one algorithm across your estate next quarter, who would own that answer?
