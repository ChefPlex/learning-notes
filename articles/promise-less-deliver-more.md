# Promise Less, Deliver More

A TPM lesson from a cryptography team: the fastest way to fix how a team was seen was to promise less.

A few years ago I was attached to a cryptography team as its program lead. Small engineering team, a lot of other teams depending on it, no program management, and a backlog about two years deep. The teams waiting on it were frustrated. Requests went in, dates came back missed.

I didn't argue about capacity. I looked at what the team had actually finished, and that told us what it could realistically take on. The requests coming in added up to far more. So I sat down with each team that depended on it and asked what mattered most right now, and we agreed on a smaller amount of work each sprint. My side of the deal was clearing anything that blocked the team, fast.

Nobody was happy at first. Nobody likes hearing that less will be promised. But the work started landing when it was promised. After about 90 days the tone changed, and within six months the same teams that had been frustrated were praising the team.

First, reset expectations with data. A record of what the team actually finished is hard to argue with. An opinion about capacity isn't.

Second, make the cap a ceiling, not a quota. When someone finished early, they took more, and that landed as a pleasant surprise instead of a missed date.

Third, throughput follows morale. The team's output had been falling because nothing it did ever came back as positive feedback. Once the work landed and people said so, it went up.

PMI now defines a successful project as one "delivering value that exceeds the effort and expense involved, as perceived by all key stakeholders." Nothing about the engineers changed here. What changed was the promise, and that changed how stakeholders saw it.

What is the smallest promise your team could make this quarter, and keep every time?
